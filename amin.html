Sample answers from recent question papers

1. What is curriculum? Curriculum is the total planned learning experiences provided by the school to achieve educational objectives. It includes content, activities, methods and evaluation procedures.

2. What is philosophy? Philosophy is the study of truth, reality and values. It influences education by guiding aims, content, teaching methods and curriculum decisions.

3. Naturalistic view on curriculum: Naturalism emphasizes nature, child development and learning through direct experience. It gives importance to the child, natural surroundings and activity-based learning.

4. Affective domain: The affective domain deals with feelings, attitudes, values and appreciation. It is important because education should develop good values, respect and responsible behaviour in learners.

5. Nature of learner: The nature of the learner influences curriculum because learners differ in age, maturity, interest, ability and readiness. Curriculum should be designed according to these differences.

6. Curriculum as plan for learning: Curriculum is planned learning. It specifies what should be taught, how it should be organized and what outcomes are expected from learners.

★ 1. Curriculum as experiences

Curriculum as experiences means that curriculum is not only a list of subjects or a textbook, but all the learning activities, situations and interactions that help the child learn. It includes both formal classroom experiences and informal experiences such as play, discussion, observation, games, field work and school activities. The teacher should organize these experiences in such a way that learners become active participants in the learning process. In this sense, curriculum is not only what is written, but what the learner actually experiences and learns from school life.

★ 2. Philosophy and curriculum in Nepal

Philosophy gives the values and principles on which the curriculum is based. It helps decide what should be taught, why it should be taught and how learning should take place. Idealism emphasizes moral values, character and spiritual development; naturalism emphasizes nature, child development and learning through natural experiences; pragmatism emphasizes utility, activity and real-life problem solving; and realism emphasizes facts, truth, science and objective reality. In Nepal, the curriculum is also shaped by democratic values, national identity, cultural diversity and the needs of local society. Therefore, philosophy acts as the foundation of curriculum planning and helps make education meaningful and relevant.

★ 3. Nature of learner and curriculum decisions

The learner is the centre of curriculum planning. Curriculum should be designed according to the age, maturity, needs, interests, abilities and differences of learners. A curriculum that is too difficult or too easy does not help the learner properly. It should match the developmental level of the child and should include activities that are meaningful and stimulating. Learners differ in intelligence, background, language and learning ability, so teachers should use different strategies and provide support according to individual needs. This makes education more inclusive, child-centred and effective.

★ 4. Aims, goals and objectives

Aims, goals and objectives are all related to the purpose of education, but they differ in their level of breadth and specificity. Aims are broad and long-term statements, such as the development of a good citizen or a responsible person. Goals are more specific and usually refer to school or subject-level outcomes, while objectives are the most specific and measurable statements of what learners are expected to achieve. For example, a teacher may aim to develop scientific understanding, set a goal to improve observation skills, and write an objective such as, “Students will identify three causes of pollution.” Thus, aims give direction, goals provide general targets and objectives guide classroom teaching and evaluation.

★ 5. Affective domain

The affective domain deals with emotions, attitudes, values, interests and appreciation. It is concerned with how learners feel and react to learning experiences. This domain includes receiving, responding, valuing, organizing and characterizing values. A learner first pays attention to a value, then responds to it, then begins to appreciate it, and gradually it becomes a part of his or her behaviour and personality. This domain is important because education is not only about knowledge but also about developing good values, respect, responsibility and social behaviour. For example, environmental education helps students develop concern for nature and the environment.

★ 6. Learning experiences

Learning experiences are the activities and situations through which learners interact with the content and acquire knowledge, skills and attitudes. A good learning experience should be relevant, meaningful and suitable to the learner’s age and ability. It should also match the objectives of the lesson and should be organized from simple to complex, known to unknown and concrete to abstract. Different experiences such as discussion, observation, project work, demonstration, experiments and field visits make learning more effective. In this way, the curriculum becomes active and child-centred rather than merely textbook-based.

★ 7. Formative evaluation

Formative evaluation is done during the teaching-learning process to improve learning. It helps the teacher know whether students understand the lesson and where they are facing difficulties. It includes class tests, oral questioning, homework, observation and classroom activities. This type of evaluation provides immediate feedback, helps teachers modify their methods and gives learners a chance to improve before final assessment. It is therefore known as assessment for learning. For example, after teaching a chapter, the teacher may ask questions, check notebooks and give short exercises to identify weak areas and re-teach them.

★ 8. Content selection and organization

Curriculum content should be selected carefully because all knowledge cannot be included in the syllabus. The selected content should be valid, useful, relevant and suitable for the learner’s level. It should also be interesting and connected with real life, local context and social needs. Content should be organized in a logical sequence so that learning proceeds from simple to complex and from known to unknown. Continuity, integration and articulation are also important, because related topics should be connected rather than treated as isolated facts. In this way, the curriculum becomes clear, meaningful and capable of producing lasting learning.

Group C – 10-mark exam answers

★ 9. Tyler’s model and curriculum development in Nepal

Tyler’s model is one of the most important models of curriculum development. It states that curriculum should begin with the identification of educational needs, then objectives should be formulated, learning experiences should be selected, these experiences should be organized systematically, and the curriculum should be evaluated to see whether the objectives are achieved. This model is useful because it gives direction to curriculum planning and ensures that teaching is purposeful. In Nepal, curriculum development follows a similar process, but it is also influenced by national policy, social needs and the National Curriculum Framework. The process includes needs analysis, preparation of curriculum draft, expert review, field testing, approval and implementation. This helps make the curriculum more suitable to the social, cultural and economic conditions of the country. Therefore, Tyler’s model is important because it provides a clear and systematic approach to curriculum development in Nepal.

★ 10. Taba’s model

Taba’s model is known as a learner-centered and teacher-oriented approach to curriculum development. According to her, curriculum planning should begin with the diagnosis of needs, where teachers identify learners’ interests, problems and learning gaps. After this, objectives are set, content is selected, learning experiences are arranged and evaluation is planned. This model is important because it emphasizes the role of teachers and the actual needs of students rather than only the abstract theory of curriculum. It is practical and flexible, and it allows adjustments according to classroom realities. In this sense, Taba’s model helps make curriculum development more realistic, relevant and useful in school settings.

★ 11. Learning theories and constructivism

Learning theories greatly influence curriculum design because they explain how learners acquire knowledge and what methods are most suitable for effective learning. Behaviourism focuses on observable behaviour and reinforcement, while cognitivism emphasizes mental processes and understanding. Constructivism, however, says that learners construct knowledge through experience, interaction and reflection. This view has changed the curriculum from teacher-centred to learner-centred. The objectives now focus on understanding, application, inquiry and problem solving, while teaching methods include discussion, discovery learning, projects and group work. Assessment also becomes more formative and performance-based. Therefore, constructivism makes learning more meaningful, active and related to real life.

★ 12. Secondary curriculum and diverse learner needs

The secondary curriculum of Nepal aims to provide students with subject knowledge, vocational orientation and preparation for higher education and work. It has many strengths, such as specialization, structured learning outcomes and continuous assessment. However, it cannot meet the needs of all learners in the same way because students differ in ability, interest, language, background and learning needs. Some learners need more support, while others need more challenge. Therefore, the curriculum should be more inclusive and flexible. It should include differentiated instruction, accessible materials, varied activities and support for children with disabilities. In this way, the curriculum becomes more equitable and relevant to diverse learners.

★ 13. Bloom’s taxonomy

Bloom’s taxonomy is a framework that classifies educational objectives from lower to higher levels of thinking. The cognitive domain includes knowledge, understanding, application, analysis, evaluation and creation. This helps teachers design objectives according to the level of learning they expect from students. The affective domain focuses on values, attitudes and feelings, while the psychomotor domain deals with physical skills and practical performance. Bloom’s taxonomy is useful because it helps teachers set clear objectives, select suitable learning experiences and use appropriate methods of assessment. In this way, teaching becomes more systematic, purposeful and capable of developing higher-order thinking among learners.

Additional high-yield 5-mark answers

14. Curriculum as a plan for learning

Curriculum is a planned and organized set of learning experiences intended to achieve educational objectives. It includes the goals of education, content to be taught, methods of teaching, activities and evaluation procedures. In this sense, curriculum is not just a list of subjects; it is a systematic plan that guides teaching and learning. It helps teachers decide what should be taught, how it should be organized and how students will be assessed. A good curriculum provides continuity, relevance and coherence in education, and makes the teaching process more purposeful and effective.

15. Pragmatism vs perennialism

Pragmatism and perennialism are two different educational philosophies. Pragmatism emphasizes practical experience, problem solving and usefulness in real life, while perennialism emphasizes everlasting truths, intellectual development and the study of fundamental knowledge. In pragmatism, the curriculum is activity-based and experience-centered, whereas in perennialism, the curriculum is more classical and theory-based. Pragmatism values learning through doing and experimentation, but perennialism values reasoning and the study of enduring ideas. Both approaches try to develop educated persons, but they differ in the type of knowledge and educational values they consider most important.

16. Elements of curriculum

The major elements of curriculum are objectives, content, learning experiences, organization and evaluation. Objectives tell us what learners are expected to achieve, while content provides the knowledge, skills and values to be learned. Learning experiences are the activities through which students interact with the content and develop understanding. The organization of curriculum includes sequence, continuity and integration, so the content is arranged in a meaningful order. Evaluation then checks whether the objectives have been achieved. These elements are interconnected, and if one element is weak, the whole curriculum becomes less effective.

17. Formative vs summative evaluation

Formative and summative evaluation differ in purpose and timing. Formative evaluation is conducted during the teaching-learning process, while summative evaluation is carried out at the end of a unit, course or academic year. Formative evaluation helps teachers improve instruction and gives students feedback for improvement, whereas summative evaluation is mainly used for grading and final measurement of achievement. Examples of formative evaluation include class tests, quizzes, oral questions and assignments, while final examinations are common examples of summative evaluation. Both are necessary, but formative evaluation is more useful for improving learning and summative evaluation is important for final judgment.

18. Inclusive curriculum for learners with disabilities

Inclusive curriculum means designing education in such a way that learners with disabilities can participate fully and meaningfully. It requires teachers to understand the strengths and needs of each learner and modify teaching methods, materials and activities accordingly. An inclusive curriculum should be flexible and should remove barriers to learning through accessible classrooms, appropriate support and assistive devices when needed. It should also allow different forms of assessment so that learners can demonstrate their understanding in ways that suit their abilities. In this sense, inclusion is not only about admitting children to school but also about giving them equal opportunity to learn and succeed.
